**A fixed deposit (FD)** keeps a lump sum with a bank for a fixed time at an agreed interest rate. **A recurring deposit (RD)** takes a fixed amount every month for a chosen period, which builds a saving habit.

- **Safety:** deposits in banks are insured by the DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (principal and interest together). Small finance banks and cooperative banks are covered too, if they are insured.
- **Interest:** fixed when you open the deposit; senior citizens often get a little more. Compare rates across banks.
- **Breaking early** usually reduces the interest; check the penalty before opening.
- **Tax:** interest is taxable as income; banks may deduct TDS above a limit unless you submit Form 15G/15H if eligible.

RDs suit monthly goals (school fees, festivals); FDs suit money you will not need for a while.
