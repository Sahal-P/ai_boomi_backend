An index fund is a mutual fund that simply copies a market index, such as the Nifty 50 or the Sensex, instead of a manager choosing shares.

- **Lower costs:** because no one picks shares, index funds usually charge less than actively managed funds.
- **Market risk remains:** when the index falls, the fund falls too. It is not a safe deposit.
- **Spread out:** one index fund holds many companies, which spreads risk compared with buying a few shares.
- **Long-term tool:** like any share-based fund, it suits money you will not need for many years.

FinBoy explains ideas but does not tell you which fund to buy. A SEBI-registered investment adviser can give personal advice.
